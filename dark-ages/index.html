Folio II · Analysis

Where Good Marketing Loses the Plot

Recurring patterns that create activity without progress, messages without meaning, or numbers without decisions.

A Treatise on Useful Motion

More activity is not the same as more progress.

Marketing breaks down when priorities drift, measurement disconnects from outcomes, internal language replaces customer language, content volume outruns strategy, or features crowd out a reason to care.

The cure is usually sharper thinking rather than greater volume: define the objective, identify the audience, establish what matters, build a system, and measure the result.

Observation I

Priority Drift

New requests keep entering because the original objective never became a real boundary.

Observation II

Measurement Without Meaning

Large numbers create confidence without explaining business impact.

Observation III

Message Dilution

Internal terminology and broad claims obscure the customer problem.

Observation IV

Content Without a System

Publishing becomes a queue of requests rather than an audience strategy.

Observation V

Feature-Led Positioning

A long feature list is not a market position.

About Matthew

A journalist’s approach to marketing: ask better questions, understand the subject, and make the answer useful.

RoleProduct Marketer / Builder

Primary ToolA Well-Framed Question

I started as a journalist, so I learned to ask questions before I learned to sell anything.

Since then I have worked across aerospace, defense, cybersecurity, SaaS, eCommerce, charitable programs, digital media, and technology products. Much of the work has involved taking something complicated and finding the story, audience, campaign, system, or experience that makes it useful.

Away from work, the questions continue. I am raising three daughters on a small Michigan farm and tend to accumulate projects involving animals, maple syrup, websites, electronics, gardens, barns, and whatever somebody decides should be built next.
